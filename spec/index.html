1.0

Purpose and scope

1.1Current state

Originate Marketing currently tracks tasks on a single flat board (outreachtaskforce.netlify.app) and distributes instructions through chat threads that cannot be searched by client, department, or date. As the team and client roster grow past what one person can hold in memory, that combination breaks down in two specific ways: a new team member cannot find the instructions that apply to them, and a department head cannot see everything happening in their department without asking around.

Originate Command replaces both with one system built around four attributes every item of work already has: a client it relates to, a department it belongs to, a person or group responsible for it, and a moment it was recorded. The system does not manage projects or campaigns in the marketing sense; it manages who was told to do what, and who was told what to know.

1.2In scope

  • Todo tracking per person, per department, per client, replacing the existing daily board
  • An instruction and notice board: any team member can post an instruction, tag it, and it becomes permanently searchable by whoever it was tagged to
  • Role based access built on a department hierarchy (department head, team lead, team member) with custom groups that cut across departments
  • An invite only account system with per person profile dashboards
  • Notifications by email, browser push, and optionally Discord

1.3Out of scope for version one

  • Client facing access. Nobody outside Originate Marketing logs into this system
  • Payroll, invoicing, or time tracking for pay purposes
  • Replacing ActiveCampaign, Instantly, or other outreach execution tools. Originate Command records what is to be done in those tools; it does not perform the outreach itself
  • A native mobile app. Version one is a responsive web app that works well in a phone browser

2.0

System overview

One screen, two panels. The left panel is what needs doing. The right panel is what people need to know. Both are filtered by the same tag system, so a client, a department, or a person always means the same thing on both sides.

2.1Left panel: Todos

A direct evolution of the existing daily board. Every todo belongs to exactly one client and one department, is assigned to a person or a group, and moves through a small set of states. Todos are grouped by person by default, matching how the team already reads the current board, with a toggle to regroup by client instead.

2.2Right panel: Instructions

A running feed, newest first, of anything that needs to be recorded and found again later: a client's standing rule, a correction, a policy, a one time notice. Each entry is short form text, tagged the same way a todo is, and it remains in the feed permanently rather than disappearing once read. The reference case is the Chaim instruction about documenting context in ActiveCampaign before a meeting is booked. An instruction is not a todo; it has no done state, though it may optionally spawn one.

Terminology used throughout this document

Todo: a discrete task assigned to a person or group, with a done or not done state.

Instruction: a standing piece of information, guidance, or notice. No done state by default.

Tag: a label attached to a todo or instruction. The four built in tag types are client, department, type, and category; custom tags can be created freely.

Group: a named set of people assembled for a purpose, independent of department. A todo or instruction can be assigned to a group the same way it is assigned to a person.

2.3The profile dashboard

Every person, on login, lands on a dashboard scoped to them: their assigned clients, their open todos across those clients, the instructions tagged to them personally or to their department, and any groups they belong to. What a department head or team lead sees in addition is covered in section 3.0.

3.2Who may create, and who may assign

Decision point

The source brief states that "any one got a new instruction and todo can do that, and then tag/assign the department." Read literally, any team member could post an instruction and assign it to any department, not only their own, which is reasonable given that instructions frequently originate with a client and must reach whichever department the request actually concerns. Recommendation: keep instruction creation and tagging open to every authenticated user, since any team member may be the one who hears a client instruction, but keep todo assignment restricted to the system admin, department heads, and team leads, so work cannot be handed to a peer outside the normal chain of command. Sections 6.2 and 6.3 are written against this recommendation.

Leadership is a tier, not one person

System Admin in table 3.1 is a tier, not a single account. It covers whoever sits above every department head: Shohag Munshe today, and Founder, CEO, or other executive roles as the company grows. Anyone in this tier sees everything and receives every escalation under section 9.0; nobody outside the tier does. No account joins this tier by default, it is granted explicitly, in the same way as department head.

3.3Department head is a grant, not a job title

One person can be department head of more than one department at once, as required by the source brief. This is modelled as a many to many relationship, in which a person holds the department head grant for zero, one, or several departments, rather than as a single fixed job title field on the user record. That is what makes it possible to add or remove the authority later without restructuring anyone's account.

3.4Custom hierarchy levels

The three levels in table 3.1 below system admin are the defaults every department starts with. A department may define additional levels between or alongside them, for example a Senior Strategist level sitting between team lead and team member in Outreach Operations, without any change to the underlying permission engine, because permissions are computed from a level's position in that department's ordered hierarchy list rather than from a hardcoded role name.

4.0

Departments and hierarchy

4.1Departments at launch

Six departments at launch, each independently structured. A person may belong to more than one department, and their level may differ between them: department head in one, team member in another.

Admin & HR internal

  • Hiring, onboarding, invites, people records

Business Operations internal

  • Process, reporting, client account health

Lead Generation client facing

  • List building, sourcing, qualification

Outreach Operations client facing

  • Sequences, replies, booking, ActiveCampaign

Social Media Management client facing

  • Content calendars, posting, engagement

Web Development delivery

  • Sites, landing pages, tracking, fixes

New departments can be created by the system admin at any time. This list is data, not a hardcoded schema, so adding Paid Advertising as a seventh department next quarter requires no development work.

4.2Groups: the cross cutting layer

A group is separate from the department tree. It exists for a case the source brief does not name explicitly but which follows from ordinary delivery work: a client project that needs one person from Outreach Operations, one from Web Development, and the account owner, for the duration of that project only. A group holds its own member list, can be assigned todos and instructions exactly as a department can, and is archived rather than hard deleted when the work ends.

Decision point

Who may create a group is one of the two open questions in section 13.0. This document is written against system admin plus department heads as the working default. That allows department heads to self serve for their own initiatives without every cross functional group routing through the account owner personally, while preventing team leads and members from creating ungoverned groups.

5.0

Data model

Ten entities. Everything else in the system is a view, a filter, or a permission rule computed from these.

5.1Entities

User people table

  • id, name, email, phone profile basics
  • avatar, title shown on assignments
  • status invited, active, suspended
  • notification_prefs per channel toggle

Department org unit

  • id, name, color for chip display
  • hierarchy_levels ordered list, customizable

Membership denormalized both ways

  • each user document holds a departments[] array of {department_id, level}
  • each department document holds a mirrored members[] array, kept in sync by a Cloud Function whenever either side changes
  • held on both sides because Firestore cannot join collections; this is the one place that tradeoff shows up directly

Group cross cutting team

  • id, name, purpose, created_by
  • members list of user ids
  • status active or archived

Client tag source

  • id, name for example Chaim, Rafa, Annette
  • primary_contact, notes
  • status active, paused, past

Tag label

  • id, label, kind client, department, type, category, or custom
  • custom tags creatable inline by anyone with post rights

Todo left panel item

  • title, description, due_date
  • assignee a user id or a group id, never both
  • state open, in progress, done, blocked
  • recurrence none, daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly
  • escalation_level none, lead, head, leadership
  • tags[] at minimum one client, one department

Instruction right panel item

  • body the text itself, as written
  • author, posted_at
  • tags[] client, department, type, category, custom
  • read_by[] per user read receipts
  • linked_todo optional, if it spawned a task

Comment thread reply

  • attaches to a todo or an instruction
  • author, body, posted_at

Audit Log immutable

  • every create, assign, reassign, tag change, and permission change
  • actor, action, target, before, after, at

5.2Mandatory tagging rule

Every todo and every instruction requires at least one client tag and one department tag before it can be posted. This single rule is what makes "filter everything from Chaim" and "show my department's open items" work reliably from day one, rather than depending on people tagging things consistently by habit.

6.0

Functional requirements

6.1Authentication and onboarding

  • Accounts are invite only. There is no public sign up page.
  • The system admin, or a department head for their own department, sends an invite by email, choosing the person's department(s) and starting hierarchy level(s) at invite time.
  • The invite email contains a single use, time limited link that expires after 72 hours. Following it lets the person set a password and complete their profile: name, phone, photo, notification preferences.
  • An unclaimed invite can be resent or revoked by whoever sent it, or by the system admin.
  • Department, hierarchy level, and group membership can all be changed after the fact by anyone with authority over that person, and every change is written to the audit log with the actor and timestamp.
  • Password reset by standard email link. Session length: 14 days on a trusted device, 12 hours otherwise, configurable by the system admin.

6.2Todo management

  • Create a todo: title, description, client tag, department tag, assignee (person or group), due date, priority.
  • Assignment rights follow section 3.0: the system admin, department heads, and team leads can assign within their scope; team members can update status on what is assigned to them but cannot reassign it to someone else.
  • Four states: open, in progress, done, blocked. Blocked requires a one line reason, visible to whoever assigned the task.
  • The default view groups by person, matching the current board; one action switches to grouping by client. A third view groups by department, for department heads.
  • "Copy yesterday's tasks" carries over the existing board's most used action.
  • Overdue todos are visually distinct and roll up into the daily report in 6.8 without needing to be flagged manually.
  • Recurrence. Every todo is created as one time by default, with a tick to make it recurring instead: daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly. A recurring todo regenerates automatically on schedule as a fresh instance with its own done state, so a weekday task such as Chaim's Manual Reply Check does not need to be recreated by hand each morning. Editing a recurrence rule changes future instances only; instances already generated keep their original history.
  • Attachments. Out of scope for version one. Todos and instructions carry text and links only; files remain wherever they already live, in Google Docs, ActiveCampaign, or ClickUp, and are linked to rather than uploaded. To be revisited if the team finds this limiting in practice.

6.3Instructions and notice board

  • Any authenticated person can post an instruction. This is deliberately not restricted the way todo assignment is, because instructions most often originate in a client conversation that any team member might be the one hearing.
  • Posting requires at minimum a client tag and a department tag, exactly as with todos, plus the free text body. Type and category tags are optional but encouraged.
  • The feed is reverse chronological by default, with a persistent filter bar covering tag, client, department, person, and date range, rather than a search box that has to be remembered and used.
  • Every instruction records who has read it. This addresses the Chaim case directly: the assigned team member has no need to chase or research the instruction, because it sits permanently under the Chaim tag, dated, and their read receipt establishes whether they have seen it.
  • An instruction can be converted into a todo in a single action, which is useful when a notice implies a specific task, such as documenting context in ActiveCampaign before a booking.
  • Instructions are never deleted, only archived, so the historical record for a client is never silently lost.

6.4Tagging, search, and filtering

  • Four built in tag categories: Client Department Type Category, plus unlimited custom tags.
  • Every tag field is both a searchable dropdown and a tick list; typing narrows the list live rather than requiring an exact match.
  • New custom tags can be created inline at the point of tagging by anyone with posting rights, and become available to everyone else immediately, so the tag list grows with real use instead of needing to be pre populated.
  • A saved filter, for example everything tagged Chaim, can be pinned to a person's dashboard so it does not have to be rebuilt on each visit.
  • Filtering by client returns every instruction and todo for that client across every department in one combined chronological view, as described in the source brief.

6.5Groups

  • Creation is restricted per the decision recorded in 4.2, defaulting to system admin and department heads.
  • A group has a name, purpose, member list, and its own tag that work can be assigned against.
  • Todos and instructions can target a group exactly as they target a person; every member of the group sees it on their dashboard.
  • Groups are archived, not deleted, when the work ends, preserving their history for reporting.

6.6Notifications

Specified in full in section 9.0. In summary: the in app feed always; browser push for anything assigned directly to a person or to a group they belong to; email as the reliable fallback channel; and an optional Discord bridge for teams that work in Discord day to day.

6.7Personal dashboard

  • On login: open todos for the signed in person, grouped by client, oldest due date first.
  • Instructions: everything tagged to that person, their department(s), or a group they belong to, with unread items surfaced first.
  • Clients: every client for which that person currently holds an assigned todo or a tagged instruction.
  • Department heads and team leads additionally see a scoped version of the daily report in 6.8, limited to their department or team, without requiring the system admin's company wide view.

6.8Reporting

  • Daily snapshot, matching what the current board already shows: clients complete, tasks complete, tasks remaining, per person status.
  • Historical log: every action, whether assigned, completed, reassigned, or instruction posted, timestamped and filterable in the same way as the live feed, supporting the "show me everything from Chaim, by date" review described in the source brief.
  • CSV export for anything a department head needs to carry into a client facing report.

8.0

Security and access control

This system will hold instructions given by clients in confidence, and details of who on the team is behind on what. It is treated as sensitive internal data from day one, not hardened after the fact.

8.1Enforced at the database, not only in the interface

Every permission described in section 3.0 is enforced by Firestore Security Rules reading a person's custom claims, that is their department memberships and hierarchy level, set server side by a Cloud Function and never editable by the client, and not only by hiding controls in the interface. A team member's account is unable to query another department's instructions regardless of what the client application does or does not render. This is the difference between "the application does not show it to you" and "the system will not give it to you", and it is the standard this document holds to throughout.

Risk: honest tradeoff of this approach

Firestore has no server side joins, so a rule that needs to establish whether a person is head of the department an instruction is tagged to reads that fact from the person's own custom claims rather than looking it up from the department record. That is fast and reliable, but it means claims must be refreshed, by a forced token refresh performed automatically on the next login or role change, whenever someone's department or hierarchy level changes. Otherwise their previous permissions can persist for a short window. The audit log in 5.1 exists partly to make that window visible if it ever matters.

9.0

Notification architecture

The source brief requires email, browser push, and Discord where feasible. All three are achievable at no cost at this scale; the mechanism and the real setup cost of each are set out below.

9.1Browser push

Standard Web Push, with no third party service or cost involved. The browser itself, with the user's one time permission, holds a subscription that the server can wake with a message even when the tab is closed. This is the fastest channel for "someone has just assigned me something" and is the one most worth getting right first.

9.2Email

A transactional email provider, for which Resend and Postmark are both good fits, sends the messages; both have free tiers well above what a team this size would consume. Email is the dependable fallback, the channel that still reaches someone who has not opened the application in a day.

9.3Discord

The simplest implementation is a Discord webhook: a channel such as #originate-command receives every new instruction as it is posted, with no bot hosting required. A more capable implementation uses a lightweight Discord bot so that notifications can be delivered as direct messages to the specific person or group tagged, rather than everyone seeing everything in one channel. Recommendation: ship the webhook version first, roughly an afternoon of work, and add the bot and direct message routing later, once the team has confirmed that Discord is in fact where these alerts should land.

9.4Escalation on overdue todos

When a todo passes its due date without being marked done, the system notifies up the assignee's actual chain of command, and only that chain, never by company wide alert. For a team member, the first step is their team lead. If the todo is still overdue the following day, their department head is added. If it remains overdue after that, the leadership tier defined in 3.2 is added. Each step adds a recipient and never removes the ones before it, so the original assignee and their lead remain in the loop even once the matter reaches leadership.

How the channels combine

Default per person notification setting: browser push and email on for anything assigned directly to that person, Discord webhook on for the team wide instruction feed. Every channel is a toggle in each person's own profile, so nobody is forced into a channel they do not use.

10.2Account handoff checklist

Two external accounts must exist before implementation can begin, and both must be created by the document owner. Account creation is not delegated, including to the delivery team. Firebase requires one additional step compared with previous projects, a payment card on file for the Blaze plan, though nothing on this list is expected to incur cost at Originate Marketing's scale.

  1. Firebase. Create a project at console.firebase.google.com; enable Authentication (email link and email/password) and Firestore Database in production mode; upgrade the project to the Blaze plan, which Cloud Functions require, as noted in table 10.1. Under Project settings, General, add a Web app to obtain its configuration object, and under Project settings, Service accounts, generate a new private key. Both are required by the delivery team.
  2. Netlify. Create a free account at netlify.com, then open User settings, Applications, Personal access tokens, and generate a token for the delivery team.

On receipt of both, all remaining work sits with the delivery team: the codebase, the Firestore schema, the security rules, and the Cloud Functions written directly against the project, and the frontend deployed to Netlify by token rather than by manual upload. A live URL is returned for testing. Credentials are not reproduced in this document and are not disclosed to any other party.

11.0

Migration plan

Which of the three approaches below to adopt is the first open decision in section 13.0. All three are compatible with the data model in section 5.0, so selecting one later does not require redesigning anything above.

  • Full replacement. Export the current board's open items, map each to a client and department tag, import them as todos, then retire the old board. The cleanest end state, but the team has no overlap window if something proves wrong on day one.
  • Run in parallel. The new system goes live for instructions immediately, as the current board has no equivalent capability and there is therefore nothing to conflict with, while todos remain on the existing board until the team trusts the new one, after which a single cutover date is set.
  • Staged preview. Build both panels, seed them with the current board's real data in a staging environment, and let the team use it for several days before committing to a migration date.

13.0

Open decisions

Two questions this document currently answers with a recommended default. Confirming or overriding each is the only thing standing between this draft and a build ready specification.

Confirmed

Backend and deployment: Firebase (Firestore, Authentication, Cloud Functions) with Netlify for hosting, and the account handoff process in 10.2. Attachments: text and links only for version one. Escalation: automatic notification up the exact chain of command on an overdue todo, never company wide, per 9.4. Client to department relationship: flexible; any number of departments may be active on one client at once, and nothing in the data model restricts this.

Decision 1 of 2 — migration approach

No default assumed. Section 11.0 sets out full replacement, parallel run, and staged preview. This bears directly on the week 8 roadmap item and warrants a considered answer rather than an assumption.

Decision 2 of 2 — who may create a group

Recommended default: system admin plus department heads, per 4.2. Team leads are excluded from the default to keep the number of live groups manageable early on. This is straightforward to widen later, as it is a permission flag rather than a structural choice.

Everything else in this document, the data model, the permission matrix, the two panel layout, and the tag system, is not waiting on anything and can proceed into design and build now.

0.0

Reading this document

This is a working draft, not a finished spec. Two architecture questions are still open (marked in section 13) and everything downstream of them is written against the recommended default, clearly labeled as such, so the rest of the document is usable while those get confirmed.

Every section is numbered like an engineering drawing set so it can be referenced precisely in conversation and in commit messages later ("see 6.3" instead of re-explaining the instruction board each time). The permission tables and data model are the two sections worth reading most carefully, since almost everything else in the system is built on top of them.

1.0

Purpose and scope

Originate Marketing currently runs task tracking on a single flat board (outreachtaskforce.netlify.app) and shares instructions through chat threads that are not searchable by client, department, or date. As the team and client roster grow past what one person can hold in memory, that combination breaks down in two specific ways: a new team member cannot find the instructions that apply to them, and a department head cannot see everything happening in their department without asking around.

Originate Command replaces both with one system built around four things every item in the company already has: a client it relates to, a department it belongs to, a person or group responsible for it, and a moment it was said. The system does not manage projects or campaigns in the marketing sense; it manages who was told to do what, and who was told what to know.

In scope

  • Todo tracking per person, per department, per client, replacing the existing daily board
  • An instruction and notice board: any team member can post an instruction, tag it, and it becomes permanently searchable by whoever it was tagged to
  • Role based access built on a department hierarchy (department head, team lead, team member) with custom groups that cut across departments
  • An invite only account system with per person profile dashboards
  • Notifications by email, browser push, and optionally Discord

Out of scope for version one

  • Client facing access. Nobody outside Originate Marketing logs into this system
  • Payroll, invoicing, or time tracking for pay purposes
  • Replacing ActiveCampaign, Instantly, or other outreach execution tools. Originate Command tells people what to do in those tools; it does not do the outreach itself
  • A native mobile app. Version one is a responsive web app that works well on a phone browser

2.0

System overview

One screen, two panels. The left panel is what needs doing. The right panel is what people need to know. Both are filtered by the same tag system, so a client, a department, or a person always means the same thing on both sides.

Left panel: Todos

A direct evolution of the existing daily board. Every todo belongs to exactly one client and one department, is assigned to a person or a group, and moves through a small set of states. Todos are grouped by person by default (matching how the team already reads the current board) with a toggle to regroup by client instead.

Right panel: Instructions

A running feed, newest first, of anything someone needs to record and have found again later: a client's standing rule, a correction, a policy, a one time notice. Each entry is short form text (the Chaim example about documenting context in ActiveCampaign before booking a meeting is the reference case) tagged the same way a todo is, and it stays in the feed permanently rather than disappearing once read. An instruction is not a todo; it does not have a done state, though it can optionally spawn one.

Terminology used throughout this document

Todo: a discrete task assigned to a person or group, with a done or not done state.

Instruction: a standing piece of information, guidance, or notice. No done state by default.

Tag: a label attached to a todo or instruction. The four built in tag types are client, department, type, and category; custom tags can be created freely.

Group: a named set of people assembled for a purpose, independent of department. A todo or instruction can be assigned to a group the same way it is assigned to a person.

The profile dashboard

Every person, on login, lands on a dashboard scoped to them: their assigned clients, their open todos across those clients, the instructions tagged to them personally or to their department, and any groups they belong to. What a department head or team lead sees additionally is covered in section 3.

3.0

Roles and permissions

Four roles. Authority flows down a department, never sideways into another department, unless a group has been explicitly built to cross that line.

Table 3.1 — Role capability matrix

System Admin
Shohag, and anyone he designates		 Everything, every department Anyone, any department, any group Full: invites, roles, departments, custom hierarchy levels
Department Head Their department(s) only Team leads and members within their department(s) Within their department: assign team leads, move members, edit hierarchy for that department
Team Lead Their team within the department Team members on their team Within their team: reassign tasks between members
Team Member Their own assignments, plus anything tagged to their department at large Nobody by default see note below None
Decision point

Your brief says "any one got a new instruction and todo can do that, and then tag/assign the department." Read literally, that means any team member can post an instruction and assign it to any department, not just their own, since instructions often originate from a client and need to reach whichever department the client's request actually concerns. Recommendation: keep instruction creation and tagging open to everyone (anyone can hear something from a client and log it), but keep todo assignment restricted to admin, department heads, and team leads, so a team member cannot hand work to a peer outside the normal chain. This is what section 6.2 and 6.3 are written against.

Leadership is a tier, not one person

System Admin above is written as a tier, not a single account. It covers whoever sits above every department head, Shohag today, and Founder, CEO, or other executives as the company grows. Anyone in this tier sees everything and receives every escalation (section 9); nobody outside the tier does, and nobody is added to it by default when they join, it is granted explicitly the same way department head is.

Department head is a grant, not a job title

One person can be department head of more than one department at once, exactly as described. This is modeled as a many to many relationship (a person can hold the department head grant for zero, one, or several departments) rather than a single fixed job title field on the user, which is what makes it possible to add or remove that authority later without restructuring anyone's account.

Custom hierarchy levels

The three levels above (department head, team lead, team member) are the defaults every department starts with. A department can define additional levels between or alongside them (for example, a Senior Strategist level sitting between team lead and member in Outreach Operations) without touching the underlying permission engine, because permissions are computed from a level's position in that department's ordered hierarchy list, not from a hardcoded role name.

4.0

Departments and hierarchy

Six departments at launch, each independently structured. A person can belong to more than one department, and their role can differ between them (department head in one, team member in another).

Admin & HR internal

  • Hiring, onboarding, invites, people records

Business Operations internal

  • Process, reporting, client account health

Lead Generation client facing

  • List building, sourcing, qualification

Outreach Operations client facing

  • Sequences, replies, booking, ActiveCampaign

Social Media Management client facing

  • Content calendars, posting, engagement

Web Development delivery

  • Sites, landing pages, tracking, fixes

New departments can be created by the system admin at any time; this list is data, not a hardcoded schema, so adding "Paid Advertising" as a seventh department next quarter requires no development work.

Groups: the cross cutting layer

A group is separate from the department tree. It exists for the case your brief did not explicitly name but that follows naturally from real work: a client project that needs one person from Outreach Operations, one from Web Development, and Shohag, for the duration of that project only. A group has its own member list, can be assigned todos and instructions exactly like a department can, and disbands (is archived, never hard deleted) when the work ends.

Decision point

Who can create a group is one of the two open questions in section 13. This document is written against Admin plus Department Heads as the working default: it lets department heads self serve for their own initiatives without every cross functional group having to route through Shohag personally, while keeping team leads and members from spinning up ungoverned groups.

5.0

Data model

Ten entities. Everything else in the system is a view, a filter, or a permission rule computed from these.

User people table

  • id, name, email, phone profile basics
  • avatar, title shown on assignments
  • status invited, active, suspended
  • notification_prefs per channel toggle

Department org unit

  • id, name, color for chip display
  • hierarchy_levels ordered list, customizable

Membership denormalized both ways

  • each user document holds a departments[] array of {department_id, level}
  • each department document holds a mirrored members[] array, kept in sync by a Cloud Function whenever one side changes
  • kept on both sides because Firestore cannot join collections; this is the one place that tradeoff shows up directly

Group cross cutting team

  • id, name, purpose, created_by
  • members list of user ids
  • status active or archived

Client tag source

  • id, name for example Chaim, Rafa, Annette
  • primary_contact, notes
  • status active, paused, past

Tag label

  • id, label, kind client, department, type, category, or custom
  • custom tags creatable inline by anyone with post rights

Todo left panel item

  • title, description, due_date
  • assignee a user id or a group id, never both
  • state open, in progress, done, blocked
  • recurrence none, daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly
  • escalation_level none, lead, head, leadership
  • tags[] at minimum one client, one department

Instruction right panel item

  • body the text itself, as written
  • author, posted_at
  • tags[] client, department, type, category, custom
  • read_by[] per user read receipts
  • linked_todo optional, if it spawned a task

Comment thread reply

  • attaches to a todo or an instruction
  • author, body, posted_at

Audit Log immutable

  • every create, assign, reassign, tag change, and permission change
  • actor, action, target, before, after, at

Every todo and every instruction requires at least one client tag and one department tag before it can be posted. This is the single rule that makes "filter everything from Chaim" and "show my department's open items" work reliably from day one, instead of depending on people tagging things consistently by habit.

6.0

Functional requirements

6.1 Authentication and onboarding

  • Accounts are invite only. There is no public sign up page.
  • System admin (or a department head, for their own department) sends an invite by email, choosing the person's department(s) and starting hierarchy level(s) at invite time.
  • The invite email contains a single use, time limited link (expires after 72 hours). Following it lets the person set a password and complete their profile: name, phone, photo, notification preferences.
  • An unclaimed invite can be resent or revoked by whoever sent it, or by the system admin.
  • Department, hierarchy level, and group membership can all be changed after the fact by anyone with authority over that person, and every change is written to the audit log with who changed it and when.
  • Password reset by standard email link. Session length: 14 days on a trusted device, 12 hours otherwise, configurable by the admin.

6.2 Todo management

  • Create a todo: title, description, client tag, department tag, assignee (person or group), due date, priority.
  • Assignment rights follow section 3: admin, department heads, and team leads can assign within their scope; team members can update status on what is assigned to them but cannot reassign it to someone else.
  • Four states: open, in progress, done, blocked. Blocked requires a one line reason, visible to whoever assigned the task.
  • Default view groups by person, matching the current board; one click switches to group by client. A third view groups by department, for department heads.
  • "Copy yesterday's tasks" carries over the existing board's most used action.
  • Overdue todos are visually distinct and roll up into the daily report (6.8) without needing to be manually flagged.
  • Recurrence. Every todo is created as one time by default, with a tick to make it recurring instead: daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly. A recurring todo regenerates automatically on schedule as a fresh instance with its own done state, so a weekday task like Chaim's Manual Reply Check does not need to be recreated by hand each morning. Editing a recurrence rule only changes future instances; already generated ones keep their original history.
  • Attachments. Out of scope for version one. Todos and instructions carry text and links only; files stay wherever they already live (Google Docs, ActiveCampaign, ClickUp) and get linked to, not uploaded. Revisit if the team finds this limiting in practice.

6.3 Instructions and notice board

  • Any authenticated person can post an instruction. This is intentionally not restricted the way todo assignment is, because instructions most often originate from a client conversation that any team member might be the one hearing.
  • Posting requires at minimum a client tag and a department tag, exactly as with todos, plus free text body. Type and category tags are optional but encouraged.
  • The feed is reverse chronological by default, with a persistent filter bar (tag, client, department, person, date range) rather than a search box you have to remember to use.
  • Every instruction shows who has read it. This directly answers your Chaim example: Rafa does not have to "chase this or research," because the instruction sits in Chaim's tag, dated, permanently, and Rafa's read receipt proves whether he has seen it.
  • An instruction can be converted into a todo in one action (useful when a notice implies a specific task, like "document this in ActiveCampaign before booking").
  • Instructions are never deleted, only archived, so the historical record for a client is never silently lost.

6.4 Tagging, search, and filtering

  • Four built in tag categories: Client Department Type Category, plus unlimited custom tags.
  • Every tag field is both a searchable dropdown and a tick list; typing narrows the list live rather than requiring an exact match.
  • New custom tags can be created inline at the point of tagging by anyone with posting rights, and immediately become available to everyone else, so the tag list grows with real use instead of needing to be pre populated.
  • A saved filter (for example, "everything tagged Chaim") can be pinned to a person's dashboard so they do not have to rebuild it each visit.
  • Filtering by client shows every instruction and todo for that client across every department in one combined, chronological view, exactly as described in your Chaim example.

6.5 Groups

  • Creation restricted per the decision in section 4 (default: admin and department heads).
  • A group has a name, purpose, member list, and its own tag it can be assigned against.
  • Todos and instructions can target a group exactly as they target a person; every member of the group sees it on their dashboard.
  • Groups are archived, not deleted, when work ends, preserving their history for reporting.

6.6 Notifications

Covered in full in section 9. In short: in app feed always, browser push for anything assigned directly to you or your group, email as the reliable fallback channel, and an optional Discord bridge for teams that live in Discord day to day.

6.7 Personal dashboard

  • On login: my open todos, grouped by client, oldest due date first.
  • My instructions: everything tagged to me personally, my department(s), or a group I belong to, unread items surfaced first.
  • My clients: every client I currently have an assigned todo or a tagged instruction for.
  • Department heads and team leads additionally see a scoped version of the daily report (6.8) limited to their department or team, without needing the admin's company wide view.

6.8 Reporting

  • Daily snapshot, matching what the current board already shows: clients complete, tasks complete, tasks left, per person status.
  • Historical log: every action (assigned, completed, reassigned, instruction posted) timestamped, filterable the same way as the live feed, for exactly the kind of "show me everything from Chaim, by date" review you described.
  • Export to CSV for anything a department head wants to bring into a client facing report.

8.0

Security and access control

This system will hold instructions clients gave in confidence and details about who on the team is behind on what. It is treated as sensitive internal data from day one, not hardened after the fact.

Enforced at the database, not just hidden in the interface

Every permission described in section 3 is enforced by Firestore Security Rules reading a person's custom claims (their department memberships and hierarchy level, set server side by a Cloud Function, never editable by the client), not only by hiding buttons in the interface. A team member's account is physically unable to query another department's instructions, regardless of what the client app does or does not render. This is the difference between "the app does not show it to you" and "the system will not give it to you," and it is the standard this document holds to throughout.

Honest tradeoff of this approach

Firestore has no server side joins, so a rule that needs to check "is this person a head of the department this instruction is tagged to" reads that fact from the person's own custom claims rather than looking it up from the department. That is fast and reliable, but it means claims must be refreshed (a forced token refresh, done automatically on the next login or role change) whenever someone's department or hierarchy level changes, or their old permissions can persist for a short window. The audit log in section 5 exists partly to make that window visible if it ever matters.

9.0

Notification architecture

You asked for email, browser push, and Discord if possible. All three are achievable at no cost at this scale; here is how each works and what it actually costs in setup effort.

Browser push

Standard Web Push, no third party service or cost involved. The browser itself (with the user's one time permission) holds a subscription that the server can wake with a message even when the tab is closed. This is the fastest channel for "someone just assigned me something right now" and is the one most worth getting right first.

Email

A transactional email provider (Resend or Postmark are both good fits) sends the actual messages; both have free tiers well above what a team this size would use. Email is the dependable fallback, the one that still reaches someone who has not opened the app in a day.

Discord

The simplest version is a Discord webhook: a channel like #originate command receives every new instruction as it is posted, no bot hosting required. A more capable version uses a lightweight Discord bot so notifications can be sent as direct messages to the specific person or group tagged, rather than everyone seeing everything in one channel. Recommendation: ship the webhook version first (an afternoon of work), add the bot and DM routing later once the team has confirmed Discord is actually where they want these alerts to land.

Escalation on overdue todos

When a todo passes its due date without being marked done, the system notifies up the assignee's actual chain of command, and only that chain, never a company wide alert. For a team member, that means their team lead. If it is still overdue the next day, their department head is added. If it is still overdue after that, the leadership tier defined in section 3 (Shohag, and any Founder, CEO, or other executive granted that tier) is added. Each step adds a recipient; it never removes the ones before it, so the original assignee and their lead always stay in the loop even once it reaches leadership.

How they combine

Default per person notification setting: browser push and email on for anything assigned directly to them, Discord webhook on for the team wide instruction feed. Every channel is a toggle in each person's own profile, so nobody is forced into a channel they do not use.

10.1 Getting from spec to a live system

Two accounts need to exist before anything can go live, and only you can create them, account creation is not something to hand off, even to me. Firebase takes one extra step compared to before, adding a payment card for the Blaze plan, but nothing on this list is expected to actually cost anything at your scale.

  1. Firebase. Create a project at console.firebase.google.com, enable Authentication (email link and email/password) and Firestore Database in production mode, then upgrade the project to the Blaze plan (Cloud Functions require it, the table above explains why). Under Project settings, General, add a Web app to get its config object, and under Project settings, Service accounts, generate a new private key. Send me both.
  2. Netlify. Create a free account at netlify.com, then open User settings, Applications, Personal access tokens, and generate one.

Once those are shared with me, everything else is mine to do: writing the codebase, setting up Firestore's schema, security rules, and Cloud Functions directly against your project, and deploying the frontend to Netlify with that token, no manual dragging files into a dashboard. You get back a live URL to test against. Nothing from these accounts is ever shown back to anyone else or written into this document.

11.0

Migration plan

Which of the three approaches below to take is the first open decision in section 13. All three are written to be compatible with the data model in section 5, so picking one later does not require redesigning anything above.

  • Full replacement. Export the current board's open items, map each to a client and department tag, import as todos, then retire the old board. Cleanest end state, but the team has zero overlap window if something is wrong on day one.
  • Run in parallel. New system goes live for instructions immediately (the current board has no equivalent for that at all, so there is nothing to conflict with) while todos stay on the existing board until the team trusts the new one, then a single cutover date is set.
  • Decide after seeing it. Build both panels, seed them with the current board's real data in a staging environment, let the team use it for a few days before committing to a migration date.

13.0

Open decisions

Two questions this document currently answers with a recommended default. Confirming or overriding each one is the only thing standing between this draft and a build ready spec.

Confirmed

Backend and deployment: Firebase (Firestore, Authentication, Cloud Functions) plus Netlify for hosting, with the account and key handoff process in section 10.1. Attachments: text and links only for version one. Escalation: auto notify up the exact chain of command on an overdue todo, never the whole company, per section 9. Client to department relationship: flexible, any number of departments can be active on one client at once, nothing in the data model restricts this.

Decision 1 of 2, migration approach

No default assumed. Section 11 lays out full replacement, parallel run, or decide after a staging preview. This affects the week 8 roadmap item directly and is worth a real answer rather than a guess.

Decision 2 of 2, who can create a group

Recommended default: admin plus department heads, per section 4. Team leads were left out of the default to keep the number of live groups manageable early on; easy to widen later since this is a permission flag, not a structural choice.

Everything else in this document, the data model, the permission table, the two panel layout, the tag system, is not waiting on anything and can move into design and build now.
